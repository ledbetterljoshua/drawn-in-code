# The Low-Tide Fair

## Three worlds (picked the third)

1. **A market strung across a gorge**: stalls on rope nets between two cliffs, baskets on
   pulleys, mist below. Strong catenary composition, but it's "a town on bridges", which is a
   cousin of the stilt town we're measuring against.
2. **A bazaar inside the ribs of a whale-fall** on the sea floor, lit by anglerfish. Too close to
   the night lane (lit from within) and dark scenes fight a wimmelbild's readability.
3. **The Low-Tide Fair.** Once a year the sea goes out farther than anyone remembers, and the
   house-snails, who carry whole villages on their shells, come down across the wet sand to meet.
   For one afternoon the flats are a fair: awnings strung between shells, a tea-house, a
   bookbinder, a fishmonger, a band. Everyone here carries their house. Picked: it's the
   strangest of the three and every piece of it (spiral shells, wet sand, small folk) is drawable.

## The five lines

- **Who lives here, and what are they doing?** House-snails (each shell a building), and the
  folk who ride them: otters, voles, moles and puffins, trading, drinking tea, flying kites,
  hanging laundry. Hermit crabs queue by size at a big empty shell for the once-a-year swap.
  A heron fishes the tide line. A bell-puffin on a post watches the water.
- **When and what weather?** The last hour of a long golden afternoon, the moment the tide turns.
  Low sun from the left, long shadows running right. The wet flats mirror the sky.
- **Where does the eye land, and where next?** Grandmother Snail, the oldest, whose shell is a
  spiral tower-town, left of centre (~38%). Then the tea-house snail right of centre, then the
  hermit-crab queue in the right foreground, then the heron and the thin bright sea on the right
  horizon, where the tide is coming back.
- **What moves when nobody touches it?** Every snail breathes and waves its eyestalks; bunting and
  laundry in one wind; gulls; kite; steam; the creek shimmers; folk do small things on their own
  clocks; the sea foams at the horizon. **The one interaction:** every snail on the flats turns
  its eyestalks to look at your pointer, and if you move fast, they all flinch them in.
- **What's the one thing only this world has?** Architecture that grows on a spiral and crawls.
  Stairs wind up the whorl, windows follow the growth lines, chimneys are pulled-out spines, and
  the whole town leaves a silver trail across the sand.

## Viewpoint

Eye-level a little above the sand: horizon at y = 380 on a 2200 × 1100 artboard. Everything
stands on that ground plane; size scales with distance below the horizon, and every shadow is the
same shear (sun low left, slightly in front).

## Block-in

Four greys. The tower reads at thumbnail size and the creek is a good bright leading line from the
near left corner, under the tower, out to the sea. But the headland sat to the *left* of the tower,
so the brightest shape in the picture was against mid-grey sky. Moved the headland's high point to
run behind the tower so the lit shell sits on the darkest far mass. Dark corners (rock lower left,
weed lower right) frame the flats.

## Round 1 (first full pass: sky, headland, flats, six snails, eleven folk groups, foreground)

The five worst things:

1. **The fair is all in a thin band in the middle distance.** Everything happens between y 560 and
   800, where a folk is 15 px tall on a desktop; the bottom third of the frame is empty sand and a
   big blank pool. A wimmelbild needs the crowd to come right down to the viewer's feet, large.
   *Fix:* move the band, bakery, chimes, kite kid, porters and the couple forward (y 900–1080), add
   more folk near the front, shrink the big pool to a few small ones.
2. **No value structure.** The pink soft-light grade and the lilac sand flatten it into one pale
   mid-light. The only dark is the corner rock. *Fix:* warm, deeper sand in the light and cool
   violet in the shadows; a darker, cooler headland behind the tower; heavier snail shadows; cut the
   grade's pink.
3. **The tower isn't a shell.** Its silhouette is one smooth cone; whorls only show as the ledges.
   The colour bands are salmon rectangles that read as bricks. *Fix:* each whorl a barrel with a
   notch at the suture so the outline scallops; the bands thin, continuous and spiral, broken by
   small flame marks instead of slabs.
4. **The snails look like grey rocks with pipes.** Their feet are hidden under the shells and the
   heads are lumps spotted with circles. *Fix:* longer feet that show in front and behind, the shell
   seated higher so a skirt shows under it, a smooth rising neck, a defined head, and skin with
   fine reticulation instead of dots.
5. **Clouds and headland are generic.** The clouds are strings of identical pearls; the headland is
   a wall of evenly spaced vertical stripes under a flat ribbon of grass. *Fix:* long flat cloud
   decks with a few lumps on top and a lit underside; bluffs and coves with horizontal bedding and a
   ragged grassy edge.

Also: the hermit-crab shells were drawn at the artboard origin (path built without the offset), and
the tea tables on round stones read as carts.

## Round 2

Fixed round 1: fair moved forward (band, bakery, kite, porters, couple, castle to y 950–1070),
audience round the band, kids chasing a crab, barrel whorls with notched sutures, longer feet and a
real neck on every snail, warmer sand, darker headland, flat-bottomed cloud decks, bunting strung
from the tower's ledges to masts, the tea-house and the band, hermit shells drawn in place, ripple
marks as fields instead of stamps.

**Critic (fresh eyes, screenshots only), top ten:** (1) density far below wimmelbild: empty flats
left-middle and right-middle, empty bottom band, empty sky; (2) aerial perspective backwards, a
milky lilac veil thickest on the *nearest* plane, no real darks, no hot accent; (3) folk are clones
in one pose, arms out like sleepwalkers; (4) light has no consequences: faint figure shadows, flat
puddles reflecting nothing; (5) ledges high above the horizon still sag as if seen from above, and
the grandmother's foot can't carry her tower; (6) ground texture procedural; (7) straight hairlines
(creek, kite string, bunting drops) read as UI guides, nothing balances the tower on the right,
corner blobs are blunt; (8) the tower's vertical growth lines read as wooden staves, no nacre, no
wet shine; (9) line weight is heavier on scenery than on the figures, which are the subject;
(10) the hermit queue is one shell stamped at stepped sizes, the far folk are brown stubs, the
clouds repeat, no narrative spine.

My own five worst, agreeing with most of it:

1. **The lilac veil.** The dune-shadow band and the grade's cool corner put a grey film over the
   nearest figures. *Fix:* delete the dune shadow, keep cool only in shadows, let the foreground be
   the richest, darkest, most saturated plane.
2. **Empty middle distance.** *Fix:* a snail race on the right (the narrative's second beat), clam
   diggers on the left, kids jumping puddles, a gull thief; a cropped beached boat in the near
   foreground.
3. **Ledge perspective.** *Fix:* every whorl's ellipse bends with its height against the eye line,
   smiling below the horizon, flat at it, frowning above.
4. **Shell reads as staves.** *Fix:* spiral cords (fine paired light/dark lines along each whorl),
   faint growth lines, a nacre sheen.
5. **Figures are the weakest-drawn thing.** *Fix:* heavier lines on near folk, stronger cast and
   contact shadows, a rest pose per folk (hands behind, on hips, holding something), body widths.

## Round 3

Did round 2's five: the veil is gone (dune shadow deleted, cool corner out of the grade); the
ledges bend with the eye line; the tower has spiral cords, faint growth lines and a nacre sheen;
near folk have heavier lines, stronger cast and contact shadows, a resting pose each (hanging, hips,
behind, folded) and their own build; new vignettes: a snail race on the right with jockeys, punters
and a judge, clam diggers and puddle-jumpers on the left, eelgrass tufts cropped by the bottom
edge; the hermits' shells are now a moon snail, whelks, a turban, a cone, a teacup and a thimble.

The five worst now:

1. **The tea shell was a target.** Concentric rings round a centred apex. *Fixed:* the coil's centre
   slides toward an offset apex as it winds in, so the outer whorl dominates like a real helix.
2. **The snails' necks are the dullest big masses in the frame.** Flat tan or grey wedges. *Fix:*
   mottled patches, a core shadow down the back of the neck, warm bounce under the chin; a smaller
   tea-house neck; thinner eyestalks.
3. **The far flats said nothing about the migration.** *Fixed:* two latecomer snails still crawling
   in down their trails, hazed, with riders who wave when you look.
4. **The creek's bright near rim reads as a white road, and its new meander zig-zagged.** *Fix:*
   rim at half strength, a smoother meander.
5. **The phone frame is half empty sky.** *Fix:* the phone draws the panorama larger (≈700 artboard
   units across) and biases less toward sky.

## Round 4

Checked every hover with before/after pairs (tower lights, tea lanterns, race cheer, band tempo,
seal, sleeper's door, bell, hermit swap, kite, laundry, castle flag). Found and fixed:

1. **The bell post — the story's trigger — had vanished** behind the enlarged tea-house. Moved it
   to the creek mouth where the tide comes in.
2. **The clam diggers were completely hidden** behind the grandmother's tail. Moved them out onto
   open sand by the book snail.
3. **A foreground grass tuft swallowed the hermit queue's hover.** Tuft hit areas cut to the blades.
4. **The tea-house neck** was a flat brown slab with a thick pair of rods. Smaller foot, thinner
   stalks, core shadow and mottling.
5. **The creek's meander** had a hairpin by the tea-house that looked drawn with a ruler: smoothed.

## Round 5

Performance first: the grandmother's foot and neck were filled live every frame (gradients through
clips over a large area), 4.6 ms of a phone frame. Each snail's foot and neck are now cached
sprites; only the skirt's muscle waves, feelers, mouth and eyestalks draw live, and the neck sprite
slides with the head's sway. Still layers re-ordered so shadows, reflections and the flats bake into
one bitmap. Headless Chromium now holds 60 fps (p90 16.8 ms) at desktop and phone sizes.

**Critic, second pass (fresh eyes, screenshots only):** (1) the middle band is still empty sand
with specks; (2) the foreground wet sand is a mauve tangle of white hairlines, darker and dirtier
than the sand behind, so the light dies where the characters stand; (3) poses still read as one arm
held straight out, and crowds don't look at their event; (4) the tower reads as stacked rings, not a
helix, and the snails' feet don't visibly carry their shells; (5) the tower's shadow is a long
unexplained purple band across the middle; (6) reed clumps stamped on round sand islands;
(7) scale questions; (8) no colour focus; (9) shells still out-line the figures; (10) the young
snail's silver trail reads as a white shaft from nowhere, the creek as a ski run.

My five worst, from that:

1. **Arms out.** The "folded arms" rest pose is a straight limb swung forward: it *is* the
   sleepwalker. *Fix:* drop it; crowds face the thing they're watching.
2. **Shadows too long to read.** A shear of 1.45 throws the tower's shadow two thirds of the way
   across the frame, so it reads as an unsourced band. *Fix:* 0.95, darker; shorter shadows that
   visibly start at their objects.
3. **The near sand.** *Fix:* fewer, softer ripples near the viewer; the near wet sand mirrors the
   sky (lighter, cooler) instead of darkening.
4. **The tower as rings.** *Fix:* steeper suture slope so the helix reads; windows tilt with it;
   shell seated higher so a thick fleshy foot shows under the whole shell.
5. **Empty middle.** *Fix:* a toppled parcel cart under the bunting, a picnic between the book
   snail and the tower, shrimpers with push-nets in the creek right of the tea-house.

## Round 6

Round 5's five done: rest poses no longer stick an arm out (crowds face the band and the race);
shadows shortened to a 0.95 shear and darkened so each visibly starts at its object; near ripples
thinned and the near wet sand now mirrors the sky; steeper sutures make the tower a helix, windows
tilt with them, and the shell sits higher on a fat, visible foot; a toppled parcel cart under the
bunting (hover: they stack it back), a picnic with a sandwich-thief gull, shrimpers in the creek.

The five worst now:

1. **The bottom edge was a flat stage-floor stripe** once I added a dry bar. *Fixed:* the dune's
   foot rises by the rock, dips in the middle so the fair shows through, rises again on the right.
2. **The corner rock** was a near-black blob with evenly spaced white dots. *Fixed:* lighter
   top facets, three fissures with lit lips, barnacles clustered along the cracks and the wet lower
   half, limpets, a patch of pink coralline crust by the pool.
3. **One cloud template.** *Fixed:* a small high puff, a long thin deck, a taller heap on the right,
   and high cirrus brushed across the top of the sky.
4. **The tea-house veranda floats** — its posts stop short of the foot. (Next.)
5. **The far headland's foot is a pink band of fog** that cuts the cliffs in half. (Next.)

## Round 7

A second hover pass (picnic, cart, shrimpers, latecomers, clams, rock pool, chimes, youngster),
which found the scene's worst remaining kind of bug: things you can't see or can't reach.

1. **The picnic sat behind the tower's shell**, and even moved out, the tower's rectangular hit box
   swallowed its hover. *Fixed:* moved to open sand; every snail now hit-tests its real shell
   silhouette (bands or coil outline, padded) plus its foot and neck.
2. **The parcel cart sat on the grandmother's head.** Moved back under the bunting, between her
   eyestalks and the tea-house.
3. **The near clams were under the dune bar.** Raised above it.
4. **The grandmother's neck had a dark smear** for a core shadow and two tubes for lower
   tentacles. Softer, wider core shadow; shorter, thinner feelers.
5. **The headland's foot fog** cut the cliffs in half. Thinner and lower.

## Round 8

**Critic, third pass (fresh eyes):** (1) ghostly half-transparent shapes in the wet sand; (2) milky
values, no dark anchor; (3) the sky says golden hour but the tower casts no long shadow; (4) stalls
and crate in flat front elevation with no top plane; (5) the tower's horn chimneys and a pole
sticking out of its silhouette read as construction errors, and the snail's head is putty;
(6) characters copy-paste; (7) dead foreground band, unreadable corner props; (8) materials;
(9) procedural scatter; (10) no legible sea, so the low-tide premise is lost.

The five I took:

1. **Ghosts in the sand.** Every snail's reflection was mirrored into *any* water below it, so the
   tower's lower whorls turned up as a mauve stain in a puddle 150 px away. *Fixed:* reflections are
   clipped to a band just under each snail, and pool edges no longer ring.
2. **No sea.** *Fixed:* a deeper blue band of sea on the right horizon with the far cumulus sitting on
   its far edge, and a bright surf line where it meets the flats.
3. **Construction errors on the tower.** *Fixed:* chimneys are copper pots seated on the ledges, the
   laundry pole is inside the silhouette.
4. **The shadow.** *Fixed:* a longer shear (1.15) and a darker shadow for the tower so it rakes across
   the sand to the right.
5. **Flat stalls.** *Fixed:* counters and the band's crate show their top planes; the crabs stand on
   the top.
Plus a light canvas grade (saturate 1.12, contrast 1.07) to lift the values out of the milk.

Not taken, and why: a full re-pose of the folk (three-quarter views, seen-from-behind) needs a
second rig; that's the biggest thing left (see "still wrong").

## Round 9

1. **The band's audience merged into one dark knot** with a flowering tuft growing through it: the
   strolling couple's path ran straight through the crowd. Couple rerouted between the stalls,
   audience and dancers spaced so every silhouette reads alone, tuft moved off the crowd.
2. Final hover, phone and frame-rate check (headless Chromium: p90 16.8 ms at 1440×900 and at
   390×844 @2x).

## Still wrong (for whoever picks this up)

- **One body, one view.** Every folk is the same upright profile rig, recoloured and rescaled. A
  three-quarter / back view and a few dedicated poses (carrying, reaching, sitting properly) would do
  more for the crowd than anything else left.
- **The near wet sand is still the weakest material.** It should be a broken mirror holding dark
  vertical reflections of the figures standing on it; right now only the snails reflect.
- **Snail heads.** The grandmother's neck reads as a big soft wedge; she needs a clearer head with a
  face, and a mantle collar where the body meets the shell's aperture.
- Under 4× CPU throttling (headless, software raster) it drops to ~15 fps; unverified on a real phone.
