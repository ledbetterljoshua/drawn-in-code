# The Low-Tide Fair

## Three worlds (picked the third)

1. **A market strung across a gorge**: stalls on rope nets between two cliffs, baskets on
   pulleys, mist below. Strong catenary composition, but it's "a town on bridges", which is a
   cousin of the stilt town we're measuring against.
2. **A bazaar inside the ribs of a whale-fall** on the sea floor, lit by anglerfish. Too close to
   the night lane (lit from within) and dark scenes fight a wimmelbild's readability.
3. **The Low-Tide Fair.** Once a year the sea goes out farther than anyone remembers, and the
   house-snails, who carry whole villages on their shells, come down across the wet sand to meet.
   For one afternoon the flats are a fair: awnings strung between shells, a tea-house, a
   bookbinder, a fishmonger, a band. Everyone here carries their house. Picked: it's the
   strangest of the three and every piece of it (spiral shells, wet sand, small folk) is drawable.

## The five lines

- **Who lives here, and what are they doing?** House-snails (each shell a building), and the
  folk who ride them: otters, voles, moles and puffins, trading, drinking tea, flying kites,
  hanging laundry. Hermit crabs queue by size at a big empty shell for the once-a-year swap.
  A heron fishes the tide line. A bell-puffin on a post watches the water.
- **When and what weather?** The last hour of a long golden afternoon, the moment the tide turns.
  Low sun from the left, long shadows running right. The wet flats mirror the sky.
- **Where does the eye land, and where next?** Grandmother Snail, the oldest, whose shell is a
  spiral tower-town, left of centre (~38%). Then the tea-house snail right of centre, then the
  hermit-crab queue in the right foreground, then the heron and the thin bright sea on the right
  horizon, where the tide is coming back.
- **What moves when nobody touches it?** Every snail breathes and waves its eyestalks; bunting and
  laundry in one wind; gulls; kite; steam; the creek shimmers; folk do small things on their own
  clocks; the sea foams at the horizon. **The one interaction:** every snail on the flats turns
  its eyestalks to look at your pointer, and if you move fast, they all flinch them in.
- **What's the one thing only this world has?** Architecture that grows on a spiral and crawls.
  Stairs wind up the whorl, windows follow the growth lines, chimneys are pulled-out spines, and
  the whole town leaves a silver trail across the sand.

## Viewpoint

Eye-level a little above the sand: horizon at y = 380 on a 2200 × 1100 artboard. Everything
stands on that ground plane; size scales with distance below the horizon, and every shadow is the
same shear (sun low left, slightly in front).
