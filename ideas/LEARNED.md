# What the factory learned

When a backlog idea becomes a scene, compare the critic's scores and note with how the scene actually
turned out, and write a line here about what the scoring missed. Newest at the bottom.

## Batch 2026-10-29-1 (4 hands, 12 pitches)

- Each trio of pitches from one hand converged on the same mechanic (three eclipse spirals, three
  "rival lantern" pairs). The critic's `distinct` score caught it. Worth trying fewer pitches per hand
  and more hands.
- The critic's best fixes were about the camera: move it to where canvas is strong (frontal, flat,
  silhouette) and away from where it's weak (curved perspective, funnels, 3D coils).

## The Overwinter, drawn (batch 2026-10-29-1, rank 1)

- **`life` 5 was right.** The contagious yawn was the easiest strong part to build and the most
  fun to use: a neighbour graph, a probability biased downhill, porters who race to settle it, and
  the bear's shudder as the payoff.
- **`drawable` 4 was generous, for a reason the critic half-saw.** Its fix (60–80 drawers of uneven
  size, foreshortened) was essential: the first block-in read as an office block with windows. What
  it missed is that the camera fights the story. A low eye looking up a tall face hides what is in
  the drawers, and a backlit subject is a dark silhouette, so every sleeper and porter has to be lit
  by small local lamps. Nearly half the rounds went into making the story readable inside that.
- **The `cliche` warning was real but aimed at the wrong risk.** It wasn't hotel-porter whimsy that
  threatened the picture. It was "a building": drawers in tiers read as a tower block until the rock
  had wandering bedding, ledges, an arch tunnel and a drum-wrapped face.
- **A light card can hide the light.** "Two light sources" plus "sunset behind the stack" put the
  key light behind the subject. Worth having the critic check that the main light can be seen.
